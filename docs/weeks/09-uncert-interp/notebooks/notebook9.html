Before the seminar

The lecture stated two results without proof: exercise 1 proves quantile optimality, and exercise 2 proves the coverage of a split conformal interval. Exercise 3 computes partial dependence and individual curves by hand. Do all three on paper before the seminar. You may use, without proof: differentiation under the integral sign (if \(G(a) = \int_{-\infty}^a h(y, a)\,dy\), then \(G'(a) = h(a, a) + \int_{-\infty}^a \partial_a h(y, a)\,dy\), and similarly for \(\int_a^\infty\), where the boundary term changes sign); and that if \(V_1, \ldots, V_{m+1}\) are independent draws from one continuous distribution, every ordering of them is equally likely.

Exercise 1 (quantile optimality). \(Y\) has a continuous distribution function \(F\) with a density \(f\) and a finite mean, and \(0 < \tau < 1\). The pinball loss of a prediction \(\hat y\) is \(\rho_\tau(y - \hat y) = (y - \hat y)\big(\tau - \mathbf 1\{y < \hat y\}\big)\). Let \(G(\hat y) = \mathbb E[\rho_\tau(Y - \hat y)]\).

  1. Before any calculus: move the prediction from \(\hat y\) up to \(\hat y + \delta\), with \(\delta > 0\) small. How does the loss change for an outcome below \(\hat y\)? For an outcome above \(\hat y + \delta\)? Use this to guess at which \(\hat y\) the expected loss stops falling.

  2. Write \(G(\hat y)\) as two integrals, one over \(y < \hat y\) and one over \(y \ge \hat y\), and show that \(G'(\hat y) = F(\hat y) - \tau\).

  3. Show that \(G\) is convex, and conclude that a \(\tau\)-quantile, a value \(q_\tau\) with \(F(q_\tau) = \tau\), minimizes \(G\). When is the minimizer not unique?

  4. Explain why the same argument shows that, for \(Y\) given \(X = x\), the conditional \(\tau\)-quantile \(q_\tau(x)\) is the best prediction at \(x\), and why the function \(x \mapsto q_\tau(x)\) minimizes \(\mathbb E[\rho_\tau(Y - h(X))]\) over functions \(h\).

NoteHint 1
  1. Below the prediction the loss is \((1 - \tau)(\hat y - y)\); above it, \(\tau(y - \hat y)\). Which outcomes are below \(\hat y\), and with what probability?
NoteHint 2
  1. The boundary terms from differentiating under the integral sign are zero: at \(y = \hat y\) both integrands vanish.
NoteHint 3
  1. A differentiable function whose derivative is nondecreasing is convex. (d) \(\mathbb E[\rho_\tau(Y - h(X))] = \mathbb E\big[\mathbb E[\rho_\tau(Y - h(X)) \mid X]\big]\).

Exercise 2 (coverage of a split conformal interval; Lei et al. 2018). A model \(\hat f\) has been fitted on training rows. Then \(m\) held-out rows \((X_1, Y_1), \ldots, (X_m, Y_m)\) and a new row \((X, Y)\) are independent draws from one distribution, independent of the training rows. Write \(R_i = |Y_i - \hat f(X_i)|\) and \(R = |Y - \hat f(X)|\), and assume these have a continuous distribution, so that there are no ties. Let \(k = \lceil 0.9(m + 1)\rceil\), assume \(k \le m\), and let \(C\) be the \(k\)th smallest of \(R_1, \ldots, R_m\). The interval is \(\hat f(X) \pm C\).

  1. Explain why, given the training rows, \(R_1, \ldots, R_m, R\) are independent and identically distributed. Show that the interval covers \(Y\) exactly when \(R\) is among the \(k\) smallest of all \(m + 1\) values, and conclude that \(P\big(Y \in \hat f(X) \pm C\big) = k/(m + 1) \ge 0.9\). What does this probability average over?

  2. Compute \(k\) and the coverage for \(m = 9\), \(19\) and \(1{,}000\). What happens when \(m = 5\)?

  3. Suppose \(C\) is computed the same way from the training rows’ residuals instead. Which step of (a) fails, and in which direction does the coverage usually move?

  4. Suppose the held-out rows were also used to choose between several models before \(C\) was computed. Which step of (a) fails?

NoteHint 1
  1. Given the training rows, \(\hat f\) is a fixed function.
NoteHint 2
  1. The interval covers \(Y\) when \(R \le C\). If \(R \le C\), how many of \(R_1, \ldots, R_m\) can be smaller than \(R\)?

Problems

Problems 1 to 5 use the normal distribution function \(\Phi\); for a 90% interval, \(\Phi(1.645) = 0.95\). If \(W \sim N(\mu, s^2)\), then \(P(|W| \le c) = \Phi\big((c - \mu)/s\big) - \Phi\big((-c - \mu)/s\big)\).

Problem 1 (the residuals of a nearest-neighbor fit). The inputs come in groups of \(k\) rows that share the same \(x\), and \(Y = f^\star(x) + \varepsilon\) with \(\varepsilon \sim N(0, \sigma^2)\), independent across rows. The \(k\)-nearest-neighbor fit predicts, at the \(x\) of a group, the mean \(\bar y\) of that group’s \(k\) outcomes \(y_1, \ldots, y_k\). A training row’s residual is \(y_i - \bar y\). A new outcome at that \(x\) is \(Y_0 = f^\star(x) + \varepsilon_0\), independent of the group’s outcomes.

  1. Find the distribution of a training residual \(y_i - \bar y\), and of the new residual \(Y_0 - \bar y\).

  2. There are many groups, and \(C\) is the 90th percentile of the absolute training residuals over all of them, so \(C\) is close to the 90th percentile of the distribution of \(|y_i - \bar y|\). Find it. Find the coverage \(P(|Y_0 - \bar y| \le C)\) as a function of \(k\), and evaluate it at \(k = 1, 2, 5, 10\) and \(50\).

  3. Instead, \(C\) comes from held-out rows: one more row in each group, not used in the fit. What is \(C\) now, and the coverage? How much wider is the interval than \(f^\star(x) \pm 1.645\sigma\), which needs the truth?

  4. In this setting, larger \(k\) gives a narrower interval with the right coverage. What does the setting leave out that stops us from taking \(k\) as large as we like?

Problem 2 (smoothing bias and the confidence interval). One input. The rows are spread evenly in \(x\), \(n\) of them per unit of \(x\), and \(Y = f^\star(x) + \varepsilon\) with independent \(N(0, \sigma^2)\) errors, \(\sigma\) known. At a point \(x_0\) the fit \(\hat f(x_0)\) averages the \(k = 2nh\) outcomes with \(|x_i - x_0| \le h\). \(f^\star\) is twice differentiable, with \(f^{\star\prime\prime}(x_0) = a \ne 0\). For small \(h\), use \(f^\star(x_i) \approx f^\star(x_0) + f^{\star\prime}(x_0)(x_i - x_0) + \frac a2(x_i - x_0)^2\), and treat the \(x_i\) in the window as spread evenly over it, so that the average of \(x_i - x_0\) is 0 and the average of \((x_i - x_0)^2\) is \(h^2/3\).

  1. Show that the bias \(\mathbb E\hat f(x_0) - f^\star(x_0)\) is about \(ah^2/6\), and that the variance of \(\hat f(x_0)\) is \(\sigma^2/(2nh)\).

  2. Find the \(h\) that minimizes the mean squared error, bias\(^2\) + variance, and show that at that \(h\) the bias is half the standard deviation of \(\hat f(x_0)\), whatever \(n\), \(\sigma\) and \(a\).

  3. The confidence interval \(\hat f(x_0) \pm 1.645\,\mathrm{sd}\) for \(f^\star(x_0)\), with \(\mathrm{sd} = \sigma/\sqrt k\), ignores the bias. What is its coverage at the \(h\) of (b)? Does more data help?

  4. With \(d\) inputs and a window of half-width \(h\) in each, the bias is still proportional to \(h^2\) and the variance to \(1/(nh^d)\). Show that at the \(h\) that minimizes the mean squared error, bias/sd \(= \sqrt d/2\), and find the coverage for \(d = 4\) and \(d = 9\).

  5. The prediction interval \(\hat f(x_0) \pm 1.645\sqrt{\sigma^2 + \mathrm{sd}^2}\) for a new outcome \(Y_0 = f^\star(x_0) + \varepsilon_0\). With one input, the \(h\) of (b) and \(k = 90\), what is its coverage?

  6. How would you change the fit used for the confidence interval to bring its coverage back to 90%, and what does that cost?

Problem 6 (permutation importance and leaving an input out). \(Y = f(X) + \varepsilon\), with \(\varepsilon\) independent of \(X\), mean 0 and variance \(\sigma^2\), and the fitted model is exactly \(f\). Importance is measured in the population. The permutation importance of input \(j\) is \(\mathbb E[(Y - f(X^{(j)}))^2] - \mathbb E[(Y - f(X))^2]\), where \(X^{(j)}\) is \(X\) with \(X_j\) replaced by an independent copy \(X'_j\) from its marginal distribution (what shuffling a column does in a large sample). The LOCO importance of input \(j\) (leave one covariate out) is \(\mathbb E[(Y - \mathbb E[Y \mid X_{-j}])^2] - \mathbb E[(Y - f(X))^2]\): how much the risk rises when the best model without \(X_j\) replaces \(f\).

  1. \(f(x) = \sum_j f_j(x_j)\), with independent inputs. Show that the permutation importance of input \(j\) is \(2\,\mathrm{Var}\,f_j(X_j)\) and its LOCO importance is \(\mathrm{Var}\,f_j(X_j)\).

  2. Two inputs, \(X_1\) and \(X_2\) standard normal with correlation \(\rho\), and \(f(x) = \beta_1x_1 + \beta_2x_2\). Show that the permutation importance of \(X_1\) is \(2\beta_1^2\) and its LOCO importance is \(\beta_1^2(1 - \rho^2)\).

  3. Take \(\beta_1 = \beta_2 = 1\) and \(\rho = 0.95\). Compute both importances for \(X_1\). Which one says that \(X_1\) hardly matters, and in what sense is each right?

Compare its slope with the slope of \(\mathbb E[Y \mid do(X_1 = x)]\).

  • World 1: \(X_2 = \varepsilon_2\), \(X_1 = X_2 + \varepsilon_1\), \(Y = X_1 + X_2 + \varepsilon_Y\).
  • World 2: \(X_1 = \varepsilon_1\), \(X_2 = X_1 + \varepsilon_2\), \(Y = X_2 + \varepsilon_Y\).
  • World 3: \(X_1 = \varepsilon_1\), \(Y = X_1 + \varepsilon_Y\), \(X_2 = Y + \varepsilon_2\).

  1. In each world, draw the graph, find \(f\), its PDP slope for \(X_1\), and the slope of the effect of setting \(X_1\).

  2. In each world, would the model on \(X_1\) alone, \(\mathbb E[Y \mid X_1]\), have a PDP with the right slope?

  3. Which of the two models predicts better, in each world?
